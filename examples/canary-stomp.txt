Annotated transcript: exhaustion is loud, an overrun is quiet
================================================================

Setup (same numbers as tests/test_core.c, test_allocator_canary_check):

    unsigned char buf[192];
    UndyingArena arena;
    unsigned char *p;

    undying_arena_init(&arena, buf, sizeof(buf));
    p = (unsigned char *)undying_alloc(&arena, 8);

Step 1  p is non-NULL: the 8-byte payload fits in the 192-byte arena, so
        undying_alloc placed [header | head canary] [payload] [tail canary].

            undying_check(&arena)      -> ok (1)

Step 2  Write only the legal payload bytes p[0] through p[7]. The fences are
        untouched.

            p[0]..p[7] = 0x11          // 8 bytes, exactly what was requested
            undying_check(&arena)      -> ok (1)

Step 3  Write one byte past the payload. p[8] is the first byte of the tail
        canary, not spare payload.

            p[8] = 0x7f
            undying_check(&arena)      -> bad (0)

Why it is quiet: the write did not crash and did not change any OS flag. It
poisoned the fence the allocator left for itself (UNDYING_CANARY 0x55414c43,
little-endian bytes "CLAU"). Only undying_check notices.

Contrast: exhaustion is loud
------------------------------
On the 512-byte arena in main.c, after a 40-byte allocation and a 96-byte
aligned allocation, a request for the whole arena returns NULL and bumps the
failure counters on the spot:

    arena alloc_a=yes alloc_b=yes exhausted=yes ... failed=1 check=ok

The NULL and failed=1 are the arena refusing up front. The canary trap stays
quiet until you ask undying_check, which is why tests call it after every
memset. Fence != payload: see docs/why-canaries.md.
